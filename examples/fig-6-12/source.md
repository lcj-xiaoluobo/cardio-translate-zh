# Fig. 6.12

The left coronary aortic sinus, proximal part of the ascending aorta, and posterolateral free wall of the right ventricular outflow are removed to show the structure behind the left coronary aortic sinus. Note the thin crest of the left ventricular summit beneath the thick left atrioventricular groove. The posterior free wall of the right ventricular outflow tract is located above the superior attachment of the right ventricle to the left ventricle<sup>2,17</sup> between the posterolateral and anterior attachments of the right ventricle. At this posterior free wall, the right ventricular outflow tract starts to detach from the basal left ventricle. This part is also referred to as the elevated infundibulum.<sup>2</sup> Posterior free wall of the right ventricular outflow tract faces the left ventricular summit, and its medial free wall is facing the proximal part of the anterior interventricular groove.<sup>2,17</sup> The left anterior descending artery skirts posterolateral to the elevated infundibulum before descending within the anterior interventricular groove accompanied by the anterior interventricular vein. Left-side entrance of the transverse sinus between the pulmonary trunk and left atrial appendage can be recognized. The left coronary aortic sinus faces thick adipose tissue of the proximal part of the left atrioventricular groove, which is located on the left ventricular summit. The proximal left atrioventricular groove contains the left anterior descending artery and left circumflex artery. This compartment, referred to as the left coronary fossa,<sup>2</sup> is surrounded by the left atrial anterior wall posteriorly, left atrial appendage laterally, pulmonary root and trunk anterosuperiorly, left fibrous trigone and left ventricular summit inferiorly, and left coronary aortic sinus medially. The anterior half of the left coronary aortic sinus and interleaflet triangle between the right and left coronary aortic sinuses are supported by the free wall of the left ventricular outflow tract.<sup>3,55</sup> The posterior half of the left coronary aortic sinus is supported by the left fibrous trigone, the firm fibrous tissue supporting the lateral part of the anterior mitral leaflet.<sup>48</sup>
